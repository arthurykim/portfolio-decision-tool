# Taxable vs. tax-advantaged accounts

Every investing account falls into one of two buckets: regular taxable accounts, or
tax-advantaged wrappers like 401(k)s and IRAs. Same investments, very different tax
bills over a lifetime.

## The individual brokerage account

A standard brokerage account is the no-strings option: open it in minutes, put in as
much as you want, take money out whenever you like, buy nearly anything. The price of
that flexibility is taxes along the way — dividends are taxed the year you receive
them, and selling at a profit triggers capital gains tax.

## How capital gains work

Sell within one year of buying and the profit is a short-term gain, taxed like salary.
Hold longer than a year and it becomes a long-term gain, taxed at lower rates. This is
why buy-and-hold is more tax-efficient than rapid trading — and why backtests (which
ignore taxes, including the ones on this site) flatter high-turnover strategies.

## Tax-deferred vs. tax-free

Tax-advantaged accounts come in two flavors. **Tax-deferred** (401(k), Traditional
IRA): contribute pre-tax, grow untaxed, pay income tax when you withdraw in
retirement. **Tax-free growth** (Roth IRA, Roth 401(k)): contribute after-tax dollars,
then never pay tax on qualified growth again. Both beat the taxable account for
long-horizon money because compounding runs uninterrupted — no annual tax drag on
dividends and rebalancing.

## Why anyone uses a taxable account

Because the wrappers have limits and locks: contribution caps, income rules, and
penalties for touching money before retirement age. Money you'll need in five years
for a house doesn't belong behind a retirement lock. A common ordering many savers
follow — capture any employer match, then max tax-advantaged space, then spill into
taxable — but the right order depends on personal circumstances this educational site
can't judge.

## One habit that helps everywhere

Whatever the account, the tax code rewards patience: fewer trades, longer holding
periods, broad funds that rarely churn. The same behavior that minimizes taxes is the
behavior long-term index investors follow anyway.
