# Asset classes and supported tickers

## SPY — S&P 500 (US large cap equities)

SPY tracks the S&P 500 index of roughly 500 large US companies. It is the classic
"growth engine" of a portfolio: high expected return, high volatility, drawdowns beyond
-50% in severe bear markets. Data in this tool covers 1993 to today.

## VTI — US total stock market

VTI holds nearly the entire investable US equity market, including mid and small caps.
It behaves very similarly to SPY (correlation above 0.99) with slightly broader
diversification.

## QQQ — Nasdaq 100

QQQ tracks the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq companies and is heavily weighted toward
technology. It has higher expected growth and materially higher volatility than SPY —
it fell roughly -80% in the 2000-2002 dot-com crash.

## VXUS — International stocks (ex-US)

VXUS holds developed and emerging market stocks outside the United States. It
diversifies single-country risk, though US and international equities are fairly
correlated in crises. Currency movements add an extra layer of volatility.

## AGG — US aggregate bonds

AGG tracks the broad US investment-grade bond market: Treasuries, agency
mortgage-backed securities, and corporate bonds, with intermediate duration. It is a
core stabilizer holding: low volatility, modest returns, and usually (not always — see
2022) low correlation to stocks.

## IEF — 7-10 year Treasuries

IEF holds intermediate-term US Treasury bonds. Pure interest-rate exposure with no
credit risk. It sits between AGG and TLT in duration and volatility.

## TLT — 20+ year Treasuries

TLT holds long-duration Treasury bonds. It is highly sensitive to interest rates: it can
rally strongly in deflationary crises (2008, 2020) and fall hard when rates rise — TLT
lost roughly -40% from its 2020 peak through the 2022-2023 rate-hike cycle. It is used
as a crisis hedge and a deflation hedge, not a low-risk holding.

## GLD — Gold

GLD tracks the spot price of gold bullion. Gold produces no cash flow, but it has low
long-run correlation to both stocks and bonds and tends to do well in inflationary or
crisis periods. Volatility is equity-like (about 15-18% annualized).

## VNQ — US real estate (REITs)

VNQ holds publicly traded real estate investment trusts. REITs offer property exposure
and income, but in crashes they behave more like equities than like real estate — VNQ
fell about -68% in 2008.

## BIL — 1-3 month Treasuries (cash proxy)

BIL holds Treasury bills and behaves like cash: near-zero volatility, returns that track
short-term interest rates. It is the "dry powder" allocation used to damp portfolio risk.
