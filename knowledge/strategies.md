# Classic allocation strategies

## 60/40 portfolio

The 60/40 portfolio holds 60% stocks (e.g. SPY) and 40% bonds (e.g. AGG). It is the
benchmark balanced portfolio: equities drive growth while bonds cushion drawdowns. Over
the last few decades it has delivered roughly 7-9% CAGR with about half the drawdown of
an all-stock portfolio. Its weakness is periods when stocks and bonds fall together, as
in 2022 when both legs lost money simultaneously.

## Three-fund portfolio

The three-fund portfolio combines US total market stocks (VTI), international stocks
(VXUS), and aggregate bonds (AGG) — commonly around 50/30/20. Popularized by the
Bogleheads community, it aims for maximum diversification with minimum cost and
complexity.

## All Weather portfolio

Popularized by Ray Dalio's risk-balancing ideas, the All Weather portfolio spreads risk
across economic environments: roughly 30% stocks (SPY), 40% long-term Treasuries (TLT),
15% intermediate Treasuries (IEF), 7.5% gold (GLD), and 7.5% commodities or cash (BIL
in this tool). It targets steadier returns with much smaller drawdowns than stock-heavy
mixes, at the cost of lower long-run growth and heavy interest-rate sensitivity.

## Golden Butterfly

The Golden Butterfly holds five equal 20% slices: total US stocks (VTI), large-cap
stocks (SPY, standing in for the small-cap-value slice in this tool), long-term
Treasuries (TLT), short-term Treasuries/cash (BIL), and gold (GLD). It aims for the
smoothest possible ride across prosperity, recession, inflation, and deflation, and
historically shows shallow drawdowns with moderate growth.

## Risk parity

Risk parity sizes positions so each asset contributes equally to portfolio risk rather
than equal dollars. Because bonds are less volatile than stocks, risk-parity portfolios
hold proportionally more bonds (often with leverage in institutional versions). A simple
unleveraged approximation is something like 40% SPY, 40% TLT, 20% GLD.

## 100% equities

An all-stock allocation maximizes expected long-run growth and maximizes pain: -50% or
worse drawdowns must be survivable, financially and psychologically. Historically it has
beaten balanced mixes over multi-decade horizons but with brutal interim losses.
